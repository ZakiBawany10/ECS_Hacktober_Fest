How to play

Buy and sell shares in stars. Read the news, watch stars evolve, and grow your net worth: your cash plus the current value of your shares.

01 / GET STARTED

Enter the market

Enter your name and choose Start simulation. You begin with 10,000 CR. Returning players can choose Resume simulation.

The first player starts the shared universe. Once running, it keeps moving for everyone.

02 / FIND YOUR STAR

Watch the signals

Left: live prices and rankings. Select a star to inspect it.

Middle: news about events and stellar changes.

Right: the selected star’s candle chart, details and order controls. Chart markers open related news.

03 / MAKE YOUR MOVE

Buy and sell

Choose Buy, then use a quantity shortcut or type an amount. Choose Sell and use the slider to sell shares you own. Manual entry supports fractional shares.

Check the estimate, then confirm your order. Your order affects the execution price, so the final cost can differ from the quote.

Know the stellar lifecycle

Stars follow different paths as they age. Their brightness, events and trading activity affect their prices. A giant or supernova can bring a dramatic change; a black hole has zero value and cannot be traded.

Events to watch

Events can change a star’s price or its future. Read the updates: the first headline does not always reveal how the story ends.

Dyson sphere
Alien construction blocks a star’s light, pushing its price down. Completion leaves it dim; abandonment can bring a sharp rebound. A construction pause does not guarantee abandonment.
Star merger
Two stars spiral together and may combine into a bigger, brighter star. Expect volatility as they approach; a near miss is also possible.
Intelligent life
A growing civilisation adds value to its star. Progress can stall, and extinction or a powerful flare can wipe out that extra value.
Mysterious dimming
Repeated dips may be passing dust or the start of a Dyson sphere. Dust clears; deeper, more regular dips can hint at something being built.
Mass transfer
One star loses gas to another. The donor tends to lose value while the receiver grows; the added mass can also change its lifecycle.
Meteor strike
A temporary dip in brightness pushes the price down, followed by a gradual recovery.
Stellar flare
A burst of brightness briefly lifts the price before fading. Flares can also wipe out young civilisations around small red stars.
Coronal collapse
An ageing giant reaches its next lifecycle stage sooner. Watch what that star is about to become.
Alien signal
A possible discovery stirs speculation. Signals may be confirmed or debunked, so a rumour is not a guaranteed gain.

Compare the news sources. Some exaggerate, some report cautiously, and some report late. Look for follow-up stories before treating speculation as fact.

Coming later: more events from the game design
Remnant supernova
A feeding white dwarf builds towards a violent price spike. It may stall, explode completely or leave a weakened “zombie” survivor.
Black hole feeding
A nearby star is stripped of material and may be destroyed or escape. The planned mechanic lets the black hole gain value; current black holes remain worth zero and cannot be traded.
Gamma-ray burst
A massive explosion sends a destructive beam through nearby systems, wiping out civilisations and triggering a wider market shock.
Magnetar starquake
A highly magnetic neutron star releases a sudden flare. The price spikes, then settles; early X-ray reports may offer a warning.
Nebula seeding
A nearby supernova changes the clues reported about a nebula, rather than directly changing its price.

Keep an eye on your holdings. A rising price only becomes cash when you sell. News can explain what is happening, but it does not guarantee the next price move.
